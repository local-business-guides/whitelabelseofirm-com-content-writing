Content writing within your agency’s SEO work

Good website copy should answer the questions a reader brings to a page and give your client a clear way to take the next step. For an agency, adding writing support can also help keep projects moving when your own team has limited time for drafts and edits.

The work might include service pages, blog articles, or updates to existing copy. The right format depends on the page’s purpose, the audience, and the information your client can provide. A clear assignment gives the writer enough direction without asking them to guess at your client’s services or voice.

A writing partner should fit your process rather than create more steps for your team. Before starting, agree on who supplies background details, who reviews the draft, and how feedback will be shared. You can also clarify how the finished copy will be handed over for your agency or client to use.

Scope, timing, cost, and useful terms

Here are the main facts to establish for a writing assignment:

  • What it covers: Confirm the number and type of pages, whether the task is new copy or an update, and any SEO details to include.
  • Typical timing: There is no single timeline for every assignment. It depends on the amount of copy, the available source material, and how quickly reviews and revisions are completed.
  • What affects cost: Scope, length, research needs, and the number of requested revisions can all affect the quote. Ask what is included before approving work.
  • How it works: Share the purpose of each page, relevant client information, and any brand guidance. Review the draft against the assignment, then send consolidated feedback.

A brief is the set of instructions and source information for a writing task. On-page SEO means elements of a page that can be improved, such as its copy and headings. Revision means an agreed round of changes to a draft.

Comparing writing support across the United States

Since your agency may work with clients in different markets, compare providers by how they handle varied audiences and client details, not by assuming one style fits every business. Look for a defined review process, clear expectations about revisions, and a way to share accurate service information. Ask how the writer avoids unsupported claims and what your team receives when the work is complete.

Check whether the provider can work from your agency’s instructions and whether the handoff fits your workflow. A useful comparison also covers communication, scope changes, and how the writer will handle gaps in the client’s source material. These points can help you assess a partner beyond the sample copy alone.

Content Writing: common questions

What information should we provide for a writing assignment?
Share the page purpose, audience, relevant client details, and any brand guidance you have. Clear source material helps the writer avoid guessing about the business.
How long does content writing take?
Timing depends on the scope, the information available, and the review process. Discuss expected delivery and feedback steps before work begins.
What affects the cost of writing support?
